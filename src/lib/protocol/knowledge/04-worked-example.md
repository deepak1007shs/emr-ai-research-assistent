# Reference 4 — A worked example at the required standard

Below is a complete, real review, rendered. **This is the standard to meet** — for
depth, tone, and specificity. Read it before composing, then produce the same
quality for the protocol in front of you.

How the rendered document maps onto the fields you return:

| Rendered | Field |
|---|---|
| The bold line under the rule | `protocol_line` |
| **As written:** … then the bullets | `title.as_written`, `title.suggestions` |
| **Correct classification:** … then the bullets | `type.classification`, `type.suggestions` |
| The paragraph above the table, then the table | `peco.intro`, `peco.rows` |
| Primary / secondary / exploratory bullets | `objectives.*` |
| **What the protocol did:** / **Verdict:** / bullets | `sample_size.*` |
| Each `### N. Heading` and its paragraph | `key_issues` |
| The closing italic line | `footer` |

What to copy from it:

- **`protocol_line` is rich.** Title in quotes, degree, candidate, department,
  institution, guide, and what else was reviewed alongside the protocol.
- **The last title suggestion is always** `Suggested full title: …`, written out
  in full as a single sentence.
- **`type.classification` runs three or four sentences** — design, allocation
  ratio, why the frame is PICO or PECO, and the reporting guideline with its
  relevant extension.
- **Suggestions quote the protocol's own words** when flagging a contradiction,
  so the student can find the line.
- **Outcomes spell out the whole chain** — what is measured, instrument, time,
  units, domain, variable type — and open with `NOTE —` when the protocol has
  left something undefined, followed by exactly how to define it.
- **The verdict is a judgement, not a label**: `PARTLY correct. …` or
  `Wrong formula. …` followed by the sentence or two that explain it.
- **Key issues are numerous and substantial** — twelve here, each a full
  paragraph that names the problem, quotes or points to it, and gives the fix.
  Do not stop at three because the protocol looks tidy.
- **Praise is allowed only where it is earned and paired with an action** — see
  key issue 12, which credits the registration plan and then still asks for the
  wording to change.

# Protocol Understanding & Review

*Design · Objectives · Outcomes · Sample Size · Key Issues*

---

**Protocol reviewed: "Impact of structured nutritional and psychological intervention on outcomes in patients undergoing elective pancreaticoduodenectomy: a randomised control trial." M.S. General Surgery thesis, Dr Vineet Kumar, Department of General Surgery, PGIMER Chandigarh. Chief guide: Dr Satish S. N. Reviewed along with the case record form and study-procedure sections of the protocol.**

## 1. Title of the Study

**As written:** "IMPACT OF STRUCTURED NUTRITIONAL AND PSYCHOLOGICAL INTERVENTION ON OUTCOMES IN PATIENTS UNDERGOING ELECTIVE PANCREATICODUODENECTOMY: A RANDOMISED CONTROL TRIAL."

### Suggestions (only what is needed)

- "Randomised Control Trial" is not the correct term. Write "Randomised Controlled Trial".
- The title says "outcomes" in general. Name the primary outcome so the examiner sees it at once: "...on postoperative complications...".
- Add the tested framework — this is a prehabilitation programme. Signalling that helps: "...structured nutritional and psychological prehabilitation...".
- Carry the corrected design into the title so it matches the methods: "...an open-label, assessor-blinded randomised controlled trial" (the body currently says "single-blinded open-labelled", which is contradictory — see Section 2).
- Suggested full title: "Impact of a structured nutritional and psychological prehabilitation programme on postoperative complications after elective pancreaticoduodenectomy: an open-label, assessor-blinded randomised controlled trial."
- The title carries the intervention and the population but not the comparator (standard ERAS) — that is acceptable in a title, provided the comparator is stated clearly in the methods.

## 2. Type of the Study

**Correct classification:** Interventional, single-centre, two-arm parallel-group, individually randomised superiority trial (RCT). The investigator assigns a prehabilitation bundle — nutritional plus psychological optimisation added to standard ERAS — against standard ERAS alone, in a 1:1 ratio. Because a treatment is assigned, the question is framed as PICO and the study should be written up to the CONSORT 2010 statement (with the non-pharmacological-intervention extension in mind, as the intervention is a behavioural/supportive bundle).

### Suggestions (only what is needed)

- The methods line "Single Blinded Open Labelled Prospective Randomized Control Trial" is internally contradictory. Open-label means no one is blinded; single-blind means one party is blinded — a study cannot be both. Your blinding paragraph in fact blinds the outcome assessors and the data analysts, which is assessor blinding. Replace the label with: "Open-label, assessor-blinded (outcome assessors and data analysts), two-arm parallel-group superiority randomised controlled trial."
- "Prospective" describes WHEN data are collected, not the design. Drop it from the design name (it is implied by an RCT).
- State in the methods that reporting will follow CONSORT 2010, and include a CONSORT flow diagram (assessed for eligibility → randomised → received allocation → followed up to 30 days → analysed) with the reason for every drop-off.
- The intervention is a bundle of two co-interventions (nutrition + psychology). State plainly that the trial tests the COMBINED prehabilitation package; it cannot attribute any effect to either component on its own.
- Serious contradiction to fix: the Ethical Justification section calls this "an observational study... there will be no change in treatment protocol or postoperative management." That is the wrong description for an RCT with an active intervention and must be rewritten (see Key Issues).

## 3. PICO (Intervention question)

The investigator assigns a treatment (a structured prehabilitation programme) and randomises patients to receive it or not, so the question is framed as PICO, not PECO. The "I" slot holds the prehabilitation bundle and the "C" slot holds standard ERAS care.

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| P — Population | Adults (> 18 years, either gender) scheduled for elective pancreaticoduodenectomy for resectable periampullary tumours, carcinoma head of pancreas, duodenal adenocarcinoma within 2 cm of the ampulla, or distal cholangiocarcinoma; ASA I/II; able to give informed consent. Excluded: pregnancy, severe cognitive impairment, major psychiatric disorders, unresectable disease, and ASA > III (note the ASA gap flagged in Key Issues). |
| I — Intervention | Structured nutritional and psychological prehabilitation added to standard ERAS, for a minimum of 4 weeks before surgery: dietary optimisation to calculated calorie and protein targets (Harris–Benedict; protein 1.2–2 g/day), multivitamins, immunonutrition (L-glutamine), probiotics and appetite stimulants; plus structured psychoeducation, pharmacotherapy where needed, and smoking/alcohol cessation, with pre- and post-operative sessions. |
| C — Comparator | Standard ERAS protocol alone — pre-operative, intra-operative and post-operative — with no added prehabilitation. |
| O — Outcome | Postoperative outcomes within 30 days: overall postoperative complications (stated headline outcome), length of hospital stay, 30-day readmission, post-operative pain, bowel-function recovery, quality of life and emotional well-being, and PD-specific complications (POPF, DGE, PPH, SSI). A single primary outcome must be chosen from these (see Section 4). |

## 4. Objectives and Their Outcomes

### Primary objective

- **To evaluate the impact of a structured nutritional and psychological prehabilitation programme on post-operative complications within 30 days of elective pancreaticoduodenectomy, compared with standard ERAS. (Written in the protocol under AIMS: "to evaluate the impact of implementation of nutritional and psychological intervention as a part of prehabilitation on post-operative complications ... within 30 days of surgery.")**
  - **Primary outcome:** Overall 30-day post-operative complication rate. NOTE — the protocol does not operationalise this: it does not say which complications count, or how they are graded. Define it as a single endpoint, for example the proportion of patients with any Clavien–Dindo grade >= II complication within 30 days, or the Comprehensive Complication Index (CCI) per patient. What is measured: presence/severity of complications. Instrument: Clavien–Dindo classification (or CCI). Time: within 30 days of surgery. Units: percentage (with 95% CI) or mean CCI. Domain: Clinical/surgical. Variable type: binary (or continuous CCI) at patient level.

### Secondary objectives and their outcomes

- To compare length of hospital stay and 30-day readmission rate between the intervention and control groups.
  - **Outcome:** Length of stay in days (continuous; from surgery to discharge) and readmission within 30 days (binary). Instrument: hospital record. Time: index admission and 30-day window. Domain: Health-service/clinical. Variable type: continuous and binary.
- To assess patient-related outcomes — post-operative pain, bowel-function recovery, quality of life and emotional well-being — between the two groups.
  - **Outcome:** Pain on a validated scale (e.g. VAS/NRS 0–10) at defined post-operative days; time to first flatus/stool for bowel recovery; quality of life by EQ-5D-5L; emotional well-being by GAD-7 and PHQ-9. Time: at T0/T1/T2 as per the protocol's schedule. Domain: Patient-reported/clinical. Variable type: ordinal/continuous/binary. NOTE — this single objective bundles four distinct outcomes; keep them clearly secondary and specify each instrument and timepoint.
- To assess PD-related complications — POPF, DGE, PPH and SSI — between the intervention and control groups.
  - **Outcome:** Each defined and graded by its international definition: POPF and PPH by ISGPS, DGE by ISGPS grading, SSI by CDC criteria. Time: within 30 days. Domain: Surgical. Variable type: binary/graded categorical. NOTE — DGE is the outcome the sample size is actually built on (see Section 5), so its status (primary vs secondary) must be settled.

### Exploratory objectives (extra analyses that can be done)

- Change in nutritional status over the prehabilitation period and after surgery — body weight, BMI, total protein and albumin, haemoglobin, MUST score and Subjective Global Assessment across T0, T1 and T2.
  - **Outcome:** Mean change from baseline in each measure between arms — describes whether the nutritional arm of the bundle actually moved nutritional status (a mechanism check), reported with 95% CI; clearly labelled exploratory.
- Change in psychological status over the same period — GAD-7, PHQ-9 and EQ-5D-5L scores across T0, T1 and T2.
  - **Outcome:** Mean change from baseline in each score between arms — a mechanism check for the psychological arm of the bundle; labelled exploratory, not powered.
- Whether the effect differs by surgical approach (open vs laparoscopic vs converted-to-open), since all three are included.
  - **Outcome:** Complication rate reported within each approach stratum, with the caveat that these are not powered comparisons — better handled by stratified randomisation (see Key Issues).
- Relationship between adherence to the prehabilitation bundle and outcome, in the intervention arm.
  - **Outcome:** Outcome by level of adherence (diet, supplements, psychoeducation sessions attended, smoking/alcohol cessation achieved) — exploratory, and requires adherence to be recorded as a variable, which the protocol does not yet do.

## 5. Sample Size — Is It Correct? Any Issues?

**What the protocol did:** The calculation is based on Ausania et al., taking the expected proportion of delayed gastric emptying (DGE) as 40.9% in the control group and 5.6% in the intervention group. Using the two-proportion power formula of Sahai & Khurshid, with Z(alpha/2) = 1.96 (alpha = 0.05, two-sided), power 90% (Z(1-beta) = 1.28) and pooled proportion p = 0.2325, the protocol obtains n = 26.9 ~ 27 per group; after a 10% attrition allowance, 30 per group, for a total of 60.

**Verdict:** PARTLY correct. The formula type is right — a two-proportion power-based calculation is the correct tool for comparing two proportions in an RCT — and the arithmetic is reproducible. BUT the study is powered on the WRONG outcome: DGE is only one of the secondary PD complications (objective 3), not the stated primary outcome of overall postoperative complications. As written, the trial is sized for a secondary endpoint and will very likely be underpowered for its own primary aim.

### Issues to fix

- Powered on DGE, not on the primary outcome. Decide the primary outcome first (see Section 4), then size the study on it. If DGE is genuinely intended as the primary outcome, say so explicitly and reword the aim to match — you cannot headline "overall complications" and power on DGE.
- The assumed effect is very large and optimistic. A fall from 40.9% to 5.6% is a 35-percentage-point absolute (about 86% relative) reduction in DGE from a nutrition-plus-psychology bundle. Justify this from prehabilitation-specific evidence, not a single external DGE figure — a smaller, more realistic effect will need many more than 60 patients, and quoting a large effect to keep n small is a common examiner criticism.
- No sample size is provided for any of the many secondary and patient-reported outcomes (length of stay, readmission, pain, bowel recovery, quality of life, well-being). At n = 60 these are exploratory — state that plainly so they are not later presented as confirmatory findings.
- The 10% attrition allowance may be low for this cohort: a 4-week pre-operative run-in plus pancreatic surgery carries real risk of drop-out, cancellation and peri-operative mortality. Justify the figure from local data, or raise it.
- The sampling technique is not named. Write "consecutive enrolment of every eligible patient scheduled for elective pancreaticoduodenectomy during the study period", and give the department's expected annual number of eligible PDs so the examiner can see that 60 is achievable within the 15-month window.
- 1:1 allocation and 30 per arm are fine as a structure — but state the allocation-concealment method alongside (see Key Issues), since a sample size without concealment is not enough on its own.

## 6. Very Important Issues to Address (in plain words)

The most important things to fix before the study starts:

### 1. The ethics section describes the wrong kind of study

The Ethical Justification page opens with "This is an observational study done for study purposes. There will be no change in treatment protocol or postoperative management." That is the exact opposite of what this trial does — the intervention arm receives a four-week prehabilitation programme that is a deliberate change to management. This looks copied from an observational synopsis. Rewrite the whole section for an interventional RCT: describe the intervention, the randomisation, the equipoise, and the fact that the control arm continues to receive full standard ERAS.

### 2. The design label contradicts itself

"Single-blinded open-labelled" cannot both be true — open-label means nobody is blinded, single-blind means one party is. Your blinding paragraph actually blinds the outcome assessors and the data analysts, which is assessor blinding. State the design the same way everywhere: "open-label, assessor-blinded, two-arm parallel-group superiority RCT", and drop "prospective" from the design name.

### 3. No single primary outcome is defined

The aim talks about "postoperative complications within 30 days", but the objectives then list length of stay, readmission, pain, bowel recovery, quality of life, well-being, POPF, DGE, PPH and SSI without saying which one the trial is built around. Choose ONE primary outcome, define it exactly (for example the 30-day Clavien–Dindo grade >= II complication rate, or the Comprehensive Complication Index), and make everything else secondary.

### 4. The sample size is powered on the wrong outcome

The 60 patients are calculated from delayed gastric emptying (40.9% vs 5.6%), which is only one of the secondary PD complications. If overall complications is the primary outcome, size the study on that; if DGE really is the primary, rename the aim to match. Either way the 35-percentage-point effect you have assumed is very large for a nutrition-plus-psychology bundle — justify it from prehabilitation data, because a realistic effect will need many more than 60 patients.

### 5. The intervention mixes two treatments, so neither can be judged alone

Nutritional optimisation and psychological optimisation are delivered together, so a positive result cannot tell you which part worked. That is acceptable provided you state plainly that the trial tests the COMBINED prehabilitation bundle. Also record adherence to each component (diet, supplements, psychoeducation sessions, smoking and alcohol cessation) as a variable — a bundle that patients do not take up cannot show an effect, and you will need this to interpret a negative result.

### 6. The ASA eligibility criteria have a gap

Inclusion allows ASA I/II, but the exclusion is written as "ASA > III", so ASA III patients are covered by neither rule. Close the gap: either include ASA I–II and exclude ASA >= III, or include ASA I–III and exclude >= IV. Choose one and write it so no patient falls between the two rules.

### 7. Randomisation and allocation concealment are not pinned down

The protocol offers two different methods as alternatives — "computer-generated random numbers OR sealed envelope". Pick one (a computer-generated sequence is the stronger choice) and describe concealment properly: sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes opened only after the patient is enrolled, with the sequence held by someone not involved in recruitment. State who generates the list, who enrols the patient, and who assigns the group.

### 8. Mixing open, laparoscopic and converted surgery will confound the result

Complication rates differ substantially by surgical approach, and all three (open, laparoscopic, conversion-to-open) are pooled. An imbalance in approach between the arms could then masquerade as an intervention effect. Stratify the randomisation by surgical approach (or restrict the study to one approach), and adjust for approach in the analysis.

### 9. The outcomes are named but not defined

POPF, DGE, PPH and SSI each have internationally agreed definitions and grades (ISGPS/ISGPF for the first three; CDC criteria for SSI); overall complications should be graded by Clavien–Dindo; pain needs a scale (VAS or NRS); quality of life and well-being already have their instruments (EQ-5D-5L, GAD-7, PHQ-9). Write each definition, its grade and the exact day it is measured into both the protocol and the case record form, so two assessors would score the same patient identically.

### 10. The analysis plan does not match the repeated-measures design

Assessments are taken at T0 (four weeks pre-op), T1 (three days pre-op) and T2 (four weeks post-op), but the statistics section lists only t-tests and chi-square at single time points. Use a repeated-measures or mixed model, or compare final values with baseline adjustment (ANCOVA), for the longitudinal outcomes. State intention-to-treat as the primary analysis. Also tidy the section — it currently mixes past and future tense and repeats several sentences.

### 11. Thirty-day follow-up may be too short for some stated outcomes

Thirty days captures the surgical complications, length of stay and readmission well, but quality-of-life and emotional-well-being benefits from prehabilitation often appear later. State clearly that 30 days is the endpoint for the primary outcome, and consider adding a 90-day quality-of-life and well-being assessment, labelled secondary, if resources allow.

### 12. Trial registration and equipoise wording

You do state that the trial will be CTRI-registered and will follow the CONSORT checklist — good; make sure registration is prospective, before the first patient is enrolled. Separately, reword the consent line "all possible treatment options will be given and none withheld", which reads oddly for a trial with a control arm: the control arm receives full standard ERAS and prehabilitation is the tested addition, so nothing standard is being withheld — say it that way.
